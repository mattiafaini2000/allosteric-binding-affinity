# Methods and evidence boundaries

## Populations and keys

The ASD allosteric-site (AS) archive, its RCSB X-ray subset, ASD Hit-to-Lead activity, complete online ASD modulator records, and PDBbind training/test tables are separate populations. An AS row is an annotation and need not identify an independent complex. The saved discovery result counts 3,102 AS rows and 2,963 unique AS PDB IDs; 2,990 rows across 2,858 PDB IDs pass the RCSB `X-RAY DIFFRACTION` method-token test, including one mixed-method entry ([snapshot](../results/reference/asd_xray_complexes_summary.json)). RCSB-unresolved records are distinct from confirmed non-X-ray structures.

Online ASD related-target records are expanded through the protein `db_serial` and domain fields. The corrected saved expansion has 242,473 domain-level interaction rows, of which 1,891 carry an AS-complex key ([online activity snapshot](../results/reference/asd_online_activity_expansion_summary.json)). A row without that key lacks a match in this mapping; it does not prove that no experimental structure exists. Online exact selected-ligand PDBbind matches have weaker structural evidence than AS-archive Tier A because the online table lacks the same chain/residue-instance information ([mapping snapshot](../results/reference/asd_online_pdbbind_mapping_summary.json)). Source rows, AS rows, PDB IDs, compounds, target domains, and interaction keys are counted separately.

## Label evidence and endpoint units

PDB-ID overlap alone is a candidate label. **Tier A** requires alignment between the PDBbind selected ligand, the ASD modulator, and RCSB structure evidence. Its recorded statuses distinguish nonpolymer instance evidence (814 AS rows), entry-level component evidence (36), and peptide chain/length evidence (25). The 875 Tier-A AS rows include 412 `Kd`/`Ki` rows and 463 other/unspecified endpoint rows ([snapshot](../results/reference/asd_pdbbind_tier_a_verification_summary.json)); the peptide and entry-level routes should not be read as stronger pose or sequence verification than their actual checks support.

**Tier B** uses matching ligand and target evidence from ChEMBL, full BindingDB, or the local BindingDB-derived file. It does not verify the crystallographic pose, assay conditions, or exact protein construct. The saved summary has 981 source rows representing 649 distinct AS rows, including 220 distinct AS rows with `Kd`/`Ki` source evidence ([snapshot](../results/reference/asd_chembl_bindingdb_tier_b_verification_summary.json)). The two BindingDB-derived source rows have unknown original endpoint type. Exact full InChIKey evidence, connectivity-only matches, parent-molecule mapping, UniProt or sequence evidence, and unresolved cases remain distinguishable in the underlying status logic.

`Kd` and `Ki` are kept apart from `IC50`, `EC50`, `AC50`, and endpoint-unknown measurements. The source routines convert supported positive measurements to pX as `6 - log10(value in µM)` or `9 - log10(value in nM)`. Original units and inequality relations are retained; a censored `<` or `>` result must not be treated as an exact equality. Source-specific rules for missing values, duplicates, preferred records, and endpoint priority remain attached to their workflow. In the online activity expansion, selected endpoints are ordered `Kd`, `Ki`, `IC50`, `EC50`, `AC50`; an exact relation is preferred. “Strict” in these records identifies `Kd`/`Ki` endpoint type, not assay independence.

The Tier-B classifier records the relation but does not require `=` for its ligand-target evidence status. Early ChEMBL recovery requests cap molecule and target results at 100 and pair-activity results at 1,000 without pagination, so that stage is not an exhaustive database survey. The later online activity expansion paginates activity requests but still uses a 1,000-record molecule-batch limit. The broad source-union script counts an absent optional Hit-to-Lead archive as zero rows, and Tier-B verification omits local BDB-derived candidates when their CSV is absent. Cache presence alone does not establish complete per-key coverage. Missing inputs, cached errors, and failed requests cannot be read as negative biological evidence.

## Structures, features, and estimator

Structure preparation distinguishes PDBbind++ prepared files, official PDBbind+ v2020 renewed archives, and an RCSB/Open Babel fallback. The fallback selects protein `ATOM` records and ligand `HETATM` records using available component, chain, and residue evidence, adds hydrogens through Open Babel, and canonicalizes ligand MOL2/SYBYL atom-type spelling. Component-only fallback, alternate copies, and nonstandard chemistry limit pose equivalence. The project-local prepared output is separate from each original archive.

Feature extraction depends on the separate DrugWise implementation's `SYBYL_GGL`, `PAIRWISE_SCORE`, and `TRIPLET_SCORE` interfaces. The downloaded model requests 11,000 selected columns: 9,902 unsuffixed, 591 ending `_x`, and 507 ending `_y` ([PDBbind run snapshot](../results/reference/pdbbind_plus_drugwise_retrain_summary.json)). The suffixes' original feature-source meaning is not established by the released feature code. Initial inference fills suffix columns from base descriptors; later experiments assign features from two separately calculated Lorentz matrices or concatenate both 11,000-column sets. These are explicit diagnostic choices, not known original training provenance.

The two Lorentz settings are `tau=1.5`, `power=2.5`, `cutoff=6.0 Å` and `tau=0.5`, `power=5.0`, `cutoff=6.0 Å`; a separate exponential-kernel diagnostic also exists. The PDBbind experiments use scikit-learn `GradientBoostingRegressor`, including a downloaded-model parameter template and named boosting presets. This is neither an XGBoost implementation nor a newly implemented graph neural network. The saved general-minus-core split has 19,158 candidate training rows, one failed feature row, 19,157 fitted rows, and 285 CASF/core test rows. Evaluation uses the general-set `pK` field for those core rows while preserving a separate `core_pk` field, so label-version differences remain unresolved.

## Recorded evaluation and limits

The [PDBbind training summary](../results/reference/pdbbind_plus_drugwise_retrain_summary.json) records core Pearson 0.8674, MAE 0.8952, and RMSE 1.1650 for the fused 11,000-column fit. The [kernel-variant summary](../results/reference/pdbbind_kernel_variant_model_evaluation_summary.json) records six further fits; its 22,000-feature concatenated variant has core Pearson 0.8678, MAE 0.8930, and RMSE 1.1649. These figures concern the 285-row PDBbind/CASF core set and the particular saved data and descriptor versions.

The ASD Tier-A appendix-overlap diagnostic contains 21 AS annotations for 20 unique PDB IDs; one PDB ID has two ASD residue annotations. The appendix and initial pretrained-inference scripts keep the first row per PDB ID for their unique-PDB summaries; the Lorentz-fusion script instead averages labels and predictions within each PDB ID. These definitions should be kept distinct when comparing runs. Local inference with the downloaded pretrained model and reconstructed features did not reproduce the reference paper's appendix predictions. A later locally retrained PDBbind model records Pearson 0.8944 after collapsing the overlap to 20 PDB IDs ([snapshot](../results/reference/asd_tier_a_retrained_pdbbind_v2020_overlap_summary.json)); a selected kernel variant records 0.9237 on that same 20-ID subset ([snapshot](../results/reference/pdbbind_kernel_variant_model_evaluation_summary.json)). These IDs belong to the paper/core test set. The overlap is therefore a diagnostic, not independent allosteric validation. Feature-selection provenance and broader target, sequence, scaffold, and binding-site generalization remain unverified.

Earlier recovery notes used broader PDB-ID-only candidate rules and produced totals that were later corrected after row-identity normalization; they should not be substituted for the Tier-A/Tier-B counts above. Raw row-level evidence, feature memmaps, fitted models, and original third-party data are absent from this public tree. Future structure prediction, SBALIGN pocket opening, DiffDock pose generation, and validation on independent ASD complexes have no completed results here.
